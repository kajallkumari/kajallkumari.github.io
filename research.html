RESEARCH

Research Experience

My research interests lie broadly in machine learning, statistical learning, and computational methods. My research experience includes neural learning architectures, graph-based learning, time-series modeling, causal machine learning, and scientific machine learning, with applications across different domains.

01

August 2026 – Present

Master’s Thesis · Idiap Research Institute, Switzerland

Machine Learning for Speech and Neurological Analysis

Supervisor: Dr. Mathew Magimai-Doss

My master's research focuses on machine learning and computational approaches to speech analysis, particularly in the context of neurological conditions. The work integrates statistical learning, speech signal processing, and computational experimentation to investigate patterns and variability in complex speech data.

The research involves developing and evaluating computational approaches to speech analysis, with an emphasis on methodological robustness and the statistical characterization of speech-related information.

02

May – August 2026 · On-site

ThinkSwiss Research Scholar · University of Fribourg, Switzerland

Machine Learning for Causal Inference in Networked Data

Supervisor: Prof. Martin Huber

Investigated machine learning approaches for modeling and inference on network-structured data, focusing on settings where observations exhibit complex dependencies. The research explored how learning-based representations of network interactions can be incorporated into statistical estimation frameworks while accounting for dependence between connected observations.

Implemented computational frameworks integrating regularized machine learning, cross-fitting, and orthogonal estimation, alongside attention-based neural weighting and neighborhood aggregation for modeling network-dependent information. Evaluated learning-assisted estimators through Monte Carlo simulations and sensitivity analyses, examining generalization, estimation accuracy, uncertainty, and robustness under varying network structures.

03

March 2026 – Present · Remote

Research Intern · Leipzig University, Germany

Statistical Machine Learning for Temporal Data

Supervisor: Prof. Gregor Weiß

Conducting research on statistical machine learning methods for identifying and modeling nonlinear relationships in temporal data, with applications to financial time series.

The work involves computational forecasting, analysis of lagged dependencies, and temporally structured model evaluation. Particular emphasis is placed on statistical validation, sensitivity to sampling windows, and assessing whether learned predictive relationships generalize across changing data conditions.

04

November 2025 – May 2026

Research Intern · IISER Kolkata, India

Graph-Based Machine Learning for Dynamic Networks

Supervisor: Prof. Tushar Kanti Nandi

Developed a graph-based machine learning framework for predicting future interactions in evolving networks, using approximately 3.69 million transaction records.

The work involved constructing temporal graph representations, extracting structural and historical interaction features, and implementing gradient-boosted learning for link prediction. Evaluated the contribution of network topology and temporal information through chronological testing, negative sampling strategies, and feature-ablation experiments.

05

August 2025 – May 2026

Research Intern · IISER Kolkata, India

Deep Learning and Foundation Models for Time-Series Forecasting

Supervisor: Prof. Saptarshi Pyne

Investigated deep learning approaches to sequential prediction, studying recurrent neural networks, transformer-based architectures, and pretrained time-series foundation models.

Implemented and evaluated LSTM, GRU, PatchTST, TimesNet, Chronos, and TimesFM, examining the influence of temporal context, model architecture, and training procedures on predictive behavior. The research emphasized chronological validation, sensitivity to input-window length, optimization stability, and generalization to previously unseen data.

06

May – July 2025

Summer Research Fellow · IIT Guwahati, India

Physics-Informed and Distributionally Robust Machine Learning

Supervisor: Prof. Natesan Srinivasan

Developed a scientific machine learning framework combining physics-informed neural networks with distributionally robust optimization.

The work integrated differential-equation constraints, automatic differentiation, and neural approximation with Wasserstein-based robustness methods and data-driven residual correction. Investigated predictive accuracy, model sensitivity, and robustness to distributional perturbations, with applications to computational financial modeling.
